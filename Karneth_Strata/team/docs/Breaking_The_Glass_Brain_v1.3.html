KS Architecture Thesis · v1.3 · Internal

Breaking the Glass Brain

A federated architecture for autonomous systems

"I love AI. We just shouldn't use it everywhere." — Greg Starns, Karneth Strata

The dominant hype in autonomy is that one large model — trained long enough on enough data — will eventually do everything: fly the aircraft, plan the mission, recognise the threat, talk to the operator. That model is a Glass Brain: polished, seductive, apparently transparent — and brittle the moment the real world hits it. This paper argues the opposite: many specialist brains, coordinated by a deterministic mission orchestrator, each wrapped in an AI Conscience, exposed as micro-API contracts, reused as lego blocks across every KS platform — including the dismounted soldier, treated as a first-class node of the federation (HNS — Human Node Soldier). None of the individual patterns are new. The argument is that composing them under a named doctrine, in the hands of a team with real safety-critical credentials, is the smart bet for multi-domain defence autonomy — and closer to how commercial aviation, Waymo, and (quietly) Anthropic actually operate than to the Glass Brain they are marketed as selling.

00 · Executive Summary

One paragraph, then the evidence.

A single large AI model running an entire autonomous platform — flight, mission, comms, threat recognition, operator interaction — is technically possible and strategically ruinous. It cannot be certified, cannot be explained, cannot be audited, cannot be upgraded piecewise, cannot gracefully degrade, cannot run comms-silent on a £50 microcontroller, and cannot be proven to not do a stupid thing. Every safety-critical industry that has survived contact with reality — commercial aviation, medical devices, nuclear control, rail signalling — long ago arrived at the same answer: deterministic software at the core, narrow specialist modules where pattern-recognition is genuinely useful, and humans visible in every decision that matters. Karneth Strata proposes to do the same for defence autonomy, with one additional named pattern: an AI Conscience — a deterministic envelope around every AI module that refuses to forward a proposal until it passes five hard gates (physical feasibility, safety envelope, mission priority, temporal coherence, confidence threshold). We love AI. We just won't use it to fly the aircraft.

Honest framing — what this thesis is

This is smart composition, not invention. Every pattern below — deterministic cores, specialist ML, orchestrator FSMs, interlocks, micro-APIs — is shipped today in aviation, automotive, medical devices or industrial control. The Karneth Strata contribution is not new technology; it is (a) the right composition for multi-domain defence autonomy, (b) a named doctrine (the AI Conscience) that makes the pattern discussable, and (c) a small team with rare safety-critical credentials executing the composition at the right moment. Serious competitors (Anduril, Shield AI, Skydio) already live in this philosophical space with far more resources. This thesis argues KS's position within the federated camp, not a unique claim to federation itself.

01 · The Commercial Case

Why we build it this way — before the engineers get a say.

The federated architecture isn't just better engineering. It is better business. Once you commit to many specialist brains with published micro-API contracts, six commercial consequences fall out of the design — and each one is a direct advantage over anyone betting on a single monolithic model.

1 Faster time to first revenue

A Tier 2 specialist (perception, SLAM, cognitive EW) can be productised and sold standalone at month 6. A federated system earns before it is "finished". A Glass Brain earns nothing until the whole thing works — and "whole" is a receding horizon.

2 Incremental feature updates

New specialist modules ship on their own cadence without touching the rest of the brain. Customers receive capability monthly, not annually. Annual retrains and full-system re-certifications are replaced by narrow, bounded module updates.

3 Bounded certification cost

The deterministic core is certified once to DAL-A / ASIL-D (mature, priced path). Each specialist is certified to its own scope under emerging mixed-criticality guidance. A new module does not reopen the core's certification — only its own. Under a monolithic approach, any change risks a full re-cert. The order-of-magnitude cost delta goes the federated way, though specific figures depend on the regulator and the module.

4 Ecosystem revenue — the AWS model

Published micro-APIs let third parties build specialist modules on the KS brain. KS takes a cut of every integration, every bundle, every platform ship. One architecture → an ecosystem of vendors. Amazon proved this is worth trillions.

5 One brain, four product lines (and counting)

UAV, UGV, PiDog and HNS all run the same Tier 1 + Tier 3 + Conscience. Single R&D investment, four addressable markets. A new platform (naval, loitering munition, sensor mast) is an adapter, not a ground-up build. That turns a drone company into a brain company.

6 Per-module pricing flexibility

Customers buy only what they need: Tier 2 Perception as a library, Tier 3 Orchestrator as SaaS, full stack as enterprise. Monolithic models force all-or-nothing. KS can price like AWS — per service, per volume, per tier — and capture customers a monolith locks out.

Cumulative revenue: monolithic vs federated (directional, 24-month view)

A monolithic "ship when it's all done" programme earns nothing for 18-24 months, then a lumpy single step. A federated product line earns from month 6, compounds with every released module, and carries less capital risk into the market.

The proposition in one sentence. The federated architecture turns Karneth Strata from a drone company into a brain company with a product line of platforms, an ecosystem of third-party modules, and a certification path that is priced rather than theoretical. That is a fundamentally different valuation multiple from a company building a single big AI model and hoping it eventually flies something.
Honest caveat — what the chart assumes

"Month-6 first revenue" assumes: customer discovery completed in parallel with build, export-control review cleared, product liability insurance bound, integration support documented, and a named pipeline customer ready to receive. Defence and commercial B2B sales cycles are 12-36 months in reality. The federated-vs-monolithic revenue gap is the defensible claim in this chart; the absolute month-6 figure is an aspirational floor, not a plan number. Year-1 defence revenue in the business model (£200-250K) requires a named pipeline — MoD/DASA engagement, NATO exercise slot, or equivalent — not a speculative forecast. Without the pipeline being real, the commercial case stands only on SaaS and hardware tiers.

02 · The Problem

The Glass Brain: beautiful, transparent, fragile.

Call a single large model that owns the whole autonomy stack a Glass Brain. The metaphor is deliberate. Glass looks transparent and attractive — and shatters on contact with anything harder than itself. In autonomy the "anything harder" is always waiting: an adversarial input, a sensor fault, a radio dropout, a jamming pattern, a battery cell that sags half a volt earlier than training predicted.

The seven failure modes below are not secret. They are simply unfashionable to list.

1 · Non-determinism

Identical inputs can produce different outputs. Re-running a mission is not re-running a mission. For safety-critical flight, this is disqualifying before the first test flight.

2 · Opacity

A 400-billion-parameter model cannot tell you why it banked left. An accident review board cannot accept "the gradients wanted to."

3 · Uncertifiable

DO-178C, IEC 61508, ISO 26262 all assume deterministic, bounded, traceable code. There is no accepted certification path for a monolithic neural policy at DAL-A / ASIL-D. None.

4 · Catastrophic forgetting

Retrain for a new sensor and you can silently break a prior capability. Safety regressions are detectable only by running the entire test universe — which no one does.

5 · Single point of failure

If the one brain wobbles, the whole platform wobbles. There is no independent second line.

6 · Compute, power, latency

A frontier LLM demands a datacentre. A tactical UAV has ~20 W of compute budget and often zero uplink. Inner-loop control needs microsecond response, not 400 ms.

7 · Adversarial fragility

Targeted perturbations — image patches, acoustic patterns, prompt injections — flip outputs with near-imperceptible inputs. In a contested environment, this is a feature of the enemy, not a bug in the lab.

The fatal property. In a deterministic system you can prove what it will not do — the altitude clamp, the airspeed envelope, the return-to-base trigger. In a Glass Brain, you cannot. You can only observe what it has not done yet. That is an empirical claim, not a safety claim, and a weapon system built on an empirical safety claim is a weapon system that will eventually surprise its operator.
Honest caveat

The pure Glass Brain described here is a limit case, not the current competition. No serious defence player is shipping a monolithic LLM as a flight controller. Anduril, Shield AI, Skydio and Waymo already operate in the federated camp. The Glass Brain argument matters because it is (a) the direction hype and external pressure push programmes toward, (b) visible in adjacent markets like end-to-end-learned automotive self-driving, and (c) a useful contrast that clarifies why the federated camp shares a discipline. The real competitive question — which federation — is addressed in §13.

03 · What Big AI is actually doing

Even the labs don't trust a single model.

It is tempting to assume that Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Meta believe in the monolithic-brain vision. They do not. Every serious AI lab has been quietly retreating from "one big model does everything" for several years. Watch what they build, not what the press releases say.

Lab / techniqueWhat it actually isWhy it matters for us
Anthropic — Constitutional AI A rule-based "constitution" evaluated by a second model to critique and revise the first model's outputs. This is a soft conscience. We propose a hard conscience: deterministic checks, not a second model that can also hallucinate.
Anthropic / OpenAI — tool use & function calling The LLM does not execute actions directly. It emits a proposal. Deterministic code decides whether to run it. They have already accepted that the model's job is to propose, not to act. Our architecture is the same contract, made explicit.
OpenAI o-series, DeepMind AlphaProof Classical search (MCTS, verifiers, theorem-provers) orchestrating LLMs as local heuristics. The frontier is not "bigger model". It is "narrow model inside classical scaffolding". Welcome to 1980s AI, better dressed.
Agentic frameworks Many calls to many specialists, coordinated by planners and verifiers, with explicit memory. Federation by another name. The industry is converging on what safety-critical engineers have always done.
RLHF, RLAIF, preference tuning Every shipped model is post-trained with an outer loop that corrects the inner model. An admission in code that the base model cannot be trusted alone. Our conscience layer is the in-vehicle equivalent.
The tell. When Anthropic publishes Constitutional AI and OpenAI ships tool use, they are telling you, in their shipping code, that they do not believe a single model is safe to act alone. If the companies who build the models wrap them in correctors, so should we.
Honest caveat — the analogy is directional, not tight

"Anthropic is moving our way" is a rhetorical flourish, not a strict technical claim. Constitutional AI uses a second model as critic — stochastic, not deterministic — which is in some ways the opposite of the KS hard-Conscience approach. Tool-use does involve deterministic tool execution, and agentic scaffolding genuinely reduces single-model authority, but the frontier labs' motivations (alignment, helpfulness, jailbreak resistance) are not the same as ours (safety envelopes, certification, EMCON survival). The shared direction-of-travel is real. The detailed architectural alignment is loose. Don't over-claim it in conversations with anyone who has actually read the Constitutional AI paper.

04 · What the autonomy industry does

Nobody ships a Glass Brain into a fielded vehicle.

Commercial aviation (Airbus, Boeing, Embraer)

Zero AI in the primary flight control path. Fly-by-wire is deterministic C, DO-178C DAL-A, triple-redundant, dissimilar-hardware voted. Decades of a 1-in-10-million fatal-accident rate achieved without a single neural network in the loop. AI is used for offline predictive maintenance and traffic modelling — never actuation.

Waymo

Specialist ML for perception (detection, tracking, segmentation). Deterministic planner (optimisation, search, rules) for trajectory and behaviour. Explicit safety envelope. Their disengagement rate (~17k miles in 2023) is the highest in the industry precisely because they refused end-to-end learning.

Anduril — Lattice

Mesh of specialist nodes with a publish/subscribe fabric. LLM reserved for operator-facing interaction ("chat with your battlefield"). The control path is not a single model. This is our closest philosophical ally in the defence market.

Shield AI — Hivemind

Deep RL policies for flight behaviour. More ML-central than Waymo or Anduril, less emphasis on formal safety envelopes. A legitimate counter-bet to ours — but one that will struggle with certification and explainability as the market matures.

Skydio

Classical visual SLAM + narrow ML perception + deterministic flight controller. Consumer/enterprise drone leader. No LLM anywhere near the aircraft. Proof that a federated approach scales to volume hardware.

Tesla FSD

Increasingly end-to-end neural. Still Level 2 after 9 years. Miles between critical disengagement in the tens to low hundreds. A cautionary tale, not a model.

Determinism vs autonomy: where the players sit

Determinism on the X-axis (how provably the system stays inside its envelope); autonomy on the Y-axis (how much it decides without a human). KS is plotted at its current (sim-proven, intent-based) position — not its destination. The philosophical corner we share with Waymo / Skydio / Lattice is the bet. Catching up the autonomy axis to those players is the work. Positions directional, illustrative of design philosophy rather than benchmarked numbers.

05 · Where AI Earns Its Seat

AI is a brilliant specialist and a mediocre generalist.

The empirical pattern across twenty years of deployed ML is painfully consistent: the narrower the task, the better AI gets. Breast MRI screening, OCR, speech-to-text, defect classification on a production line, acoustic-event detection, object detection in aerial imagery — all of these are solved, robust, shipped, and in many cases super-human. They are also all narrow. They have one input distribution, one output space, and a human-defined error cost.

The moment the task widens — "drive this car in every country", "fly this drone through any weather", "plan any mission" — reliability falls off a cliff. This is not a bug of current models. It is the structure of the problem.

Task breadth vs AI reliability (directional)

Narrow, well-defined tasks: AI is shipped, audited, and in many cases exceeds human accuracy. Broad, open-ended tasks: reliability collapses. The KS design rule: use AI where the bar is already cleared.

Where AI wins

  • Object detection / classification (EO, IR, radar)
  • Visual SLAM & scene reconstruction (specialist NNs)
  • Acoustic event classification (drones, rotors, gunfire)
  • Anomaly detection on sensor streams
  • Text summarisation / operator chat / mission debrief
  • Cognitive EW: characterising unknown emitters
  • Predictive maintenance / health monitoring

Where AI fails

  • Inner-loop flight / drive control
  • Safety envelope enforcement (altitude, airspeed, geofence)
  • Failsafe triggers (RTB, emergency land, self-destruct)
  • Mission priority arbitration (the "get the data home at all costs" ladder)
  • Cryptographic key handling & authority gating
  • Provable-correctness timing (CAN bus, actuator loops)
  • Anything a regulator must sign off in writing
The right mental model. Treat a specialist AI module the way you would treat a sensor: it produces a reading, the reading has a known error distribution, and the downstream code decides how much to trust it. A radar altimeter is not allowed to fly the aircraft. Neither is a neural network.
06 · The KS Federated Brain

Four tiers. Clear contracts. Deterministic core.

The KS brain is a federation. Four tiers, each with a defined contract, each owning the decisions it is actually good at, each incapable of reaching past its contract to override the others.

Four tiers, strict boundaries, lower tier always wins

TIER 1 Flight / Drive Core

Deterministic, MISRA C, DAL-A-candidate. Inner-loop stabilisation, actuator control, hard safety envelope (altitude, airspeed, bank, battery reserve, geofence). Runs on STM32-class MCU. Cannot be overridden by any higher tier. Same code pattern across UAV, UGV and walker — different actuator maps, identical safety contract.

TIER 2 Specialist AI Modules

Each module does one thing. Known input distribution, known output space, published error characteristics, deterministic fallback on low confidence. Every module's output passes through a Conscience before it is allowed to influence Tier 3. Modules are independently swappable — retraining perception does not touch SLAM.

TIER 3 Mission Orchestrator

Deterministic finite-state machine. Owns the priority ladder (SURVIVE > EXFIL > RTB > MISSION > OPPORTUNISTIC). Allocates tasks across a swarm. Handles failsafe escalation. No runtime learning. Every transition is logged, every decision is reproducible, every new behaviour is a change-controlled code delta — not a retrain.

TIER 4 LLM Interface (optional)

Natural-language tasking, mission debrief, chat-ops. Emits proposals that Tier 3 validates. Physically cannot issue control commands. May be absent entirely (EMCON, no uplink). The Glass Brain, safely caged to the one job it is actually good at.

The priority ladder — "get the data home at all costs"

Tier 3's priority ladder is the heart of the system. Every proposal from any module is evaluated against this stack. Lower numbers win absolutely.

The inversion. In a Glass-Brain world the LLM is the centre and everything else is a tool it calls. In the KS world, deterministic code is the centre and the LLM is a tool the operator uses to talk to it. That one inversion decides certifiability, explainability, survivability and cost.
How the architecture is delivered — the lead-box methodology

The federation argued for above is not just a runtime architecture; it is a development architecture. Karneth Strata builds, trains, fine-tunes and reviews every model and every line of code in a lead-box methodology — a locally-hosted, network-isolated compute environment in which models, weights, prompts, code and customer data never leave the box. Sovereignty is not retrofitted at procurement; it is the day-to-day shape of the engineering. Tier 2 specialists are open-weight, locally hosted; Tier 4 LLM assistance during development is permitted only under a phased tooling policy that switches to fully siloed before any customer-touching code. The internal companion documents (KS Lead-Box Methodology and KS AI Tooling Policy) carry the full hardware spec, phase rules and procurement plan; they are governance artefacts rather than portal publications.

07 · The Signature Pattern

The AI Conscience.

Every specialist AI module in the KS brain is wrapped in the same deterministic envelope. We call it the Conscience. It is not a second AI. It is not a sanity-check heuristic. It is a hard, auditable, code-reviewed contract that sits between the AI's output and anything the vehicle actually does.

The five gates, stated formally

GateQuestionExample (perception proposing a track)
1 · Physical feasibilityIs what the AI just proposed physically achievable with this airframe, this battery, this payload?"Bank 80° at 3 m/s" → reject. The airframe stalls.
2 · Safety envelopeDoes the proposed action stay within pre-defined bounds (altitude, airspeed, geofence, battery reserve)?"Descend to 5 m" in a 50 m minimum AGL zone → reject.
3 · Mission priorityDoes a higher-priority directive currently override this action? (SURVIVE > EXFIL > RTB > MISSION.)"Investigate anomaly" while in low-battery RTB → reject.
4 · Temporal coherenceIs this consistent with the last N decisions, or is the AI flipping?"Target acquired / target lost / target acquired / target lost" at 4 Hz → suppress, wait for stability.
5 · Confidence thresholdIs the AI's own confidence above the task-specific threshold for this action class?Weapons release at <0.98 classifier confidence → reject. Logging at <0.6 → fine.
The Conscience is not novel — it is overdue. Flight control systems have had these gates since fly-by-wire. Aviation calls them limit protections. Medical devices call them interlocks. Industrial control calls them safety PLCs. We are simply insisting that AI outputs are subject to the same contract as any other sensor or actuator proposal — and giving it a name that explains what it is for.
Hard vs soft conscience. Anthropic's Constitutional AI uses a second model to critique the first. That is a soft conscience — persuasive but stochastic. The KS Conscience is hard — deterministic code, unit-testable, change-controlled, certifiable. A second model can hallucinate its critique. A sign-off sheet cannot.
08 · Feasibility & Precedent

Is any of this actually new? No — and that's the point.

Every component of the KS Federated Brain already exists in shipped, certified, field-proven form somewhere. What is novel is the unified multi-domain framing and the explicit naming of the Conscience pattern. The engineering risk is execution, not invention.

Theoretical soundness

Control theory — Lyapunov stability, passivity, robust control — provides provable guarantees when the deterministic core is the ultimate authority. These guarantees collapse under end-to-end learned policies because the policy cannot be expressed as a bounded, analysable function. The KS architecture sits on the right side of this line: the core is analysable, the specialists are optional, the conscience enforces the envelope. Stability proofs apply.

What is proven today

  • Deterministic flight control — 50+ years of certified deployment
  • Specialist CNN/transformer perception — widely shipped since ~2015
  • Deterministic mission orchestrators — commercial ATC, Waymo, Anduril
  • Interlock / monitor-actuator safety patterns — IEC 61508, ISO 26262, DO-178C
  • DDS / gRPC / MAVLink service buses — daily-driver infrastructure
  • Post-quantum cryptography in the comms layer (KS, April 2026)

What is novel (= KS contribution)

  • The name: AI Conscience — a single memorable term for the interlock pattern applied to AI
  • Five-gate formal contract (physical / envelope / priority / temporal / confidence)
  • Unified multi-domain framing across UAV, UGV, walker — same brain, different actuators
  • Explicit inversion: LLM is a tool for the deterministic core, not vice versa
  • Published micro-API contracts for every block (see §09)
Bottom line. The thesis is not a research bet. It is an integration bet. Every part has shipped. The engineering work is to compose them cleanly, name the pattern, publish the contracts, and certify the whole. Karneth Strata has already done the hardest of those parts (safety-critical embedded, swarm orchestration, PQC comms) in adjacent programmes.
Honest caveat — composition is work

"Every tier has shipped" is true in different domains. Composing a DAL-A-candidate flight core, ML perception, a deterministic orchestrator and an LLM interface into one certifiable defence autonomous system has not been done end-to-end by a small company. The integration is itself the engineering challenge, and mixed-criticality certification (deterministic code + ML modules against one authority) is an open research question for EASA / FAA / MoD — guidance is still in draft. Claiming each tier is individually shipped is not the same as claiming the composed whole is easy. KS is an early mover in a maturing space, not a participant in a priced, turnkey one.

08·Δ · SWaP & Edge-Compute Physics

You cannot fly a Glass Brain — not metaphorically, literally.

The previous sections argued that a monolithic frontier model is a bad choice of autonomy architecture. This one makes a narrower, harder claim: on a Group 1–3 UAV (under ~1,320 lb MGTOWSW1), a frontier-capability monolithic model cannot physically fit the Size, Weight and Power (SWaP) envelope. Not "would be inefficient". Not "would overheat". Cannot. The gap between what flies and what serves Claude-class inference is two to three orders of magnitude on the power axis alone. The federated architecture is not a design preference; it is what the physics forces. The honest caveats — where compressed and specialist models do fit — are set out below.

The drone SWaP envelope

DoD UAS classifications put Group 1 below 20 lb MGTOW, Group 2 at 21–55 lb, Group 3 at 55–1,320 lbSW1. Total electrical budget on the airframe is dominated by propulsion; the share left for compute is what matters here. Representative public numbers:

  • Group 1 — quadrotor / small VTOL. Skydio X10 carries an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin GPUSW2; its 156 Wh battery yields up to 40 min flightSW3. Compute budget ~10–20 W. Anduril's Ghost class advertises "32 trillion operations per second" on an auto-grade NVIDIA chip costing "low hundreds of dollars"SW4 — Orin-Nano / Orin-NX class, ~15–25 W.
  • Group 2 — tactical VTOL. Shield AI V-BAT exposes 600 W for payload integration; all autonomy runs onboard via HivemindSW5. A share of that payload envelope is typically compute: ~50–150 W available for NN inference hardware, comparable to a Jetson AGX Orin at 15–75 WSW6.
  • Group 3 — larger tactical UAV. Compute budgets scale to ~100–300 W depending on fuselage power and cooling; still an order of magnitude short of a single datacentre GPU.

The airborne ceiling for a Group 1–3 compute module is therefore under ~300 W on a generous day, with most fleet members living under ~25 W. These are not theoretical limits; they are the numbers on the platforms that are flying today.

The frontier-LLM inference envelope

A single NVIDIA H100 SXM has a 700 W configurable TDPSW7. A single H100 cannot hold a 70B Llama model at bf16: the weights alone are ~140 GB and one H100 has 80 GB of HBM3, so a minimum of 2×H100 is required at bf16, with production serving guidance calling for 4×H100 to accommodate KV cache and 8×H100 to serve the full 128k context lengthSW8. That is 2,800–5,600 W of GPU power before counting CPU, NVMe, networking and cooling, which typically add another 40–80% at rack level.

Energy-per-token estimates from public research are more contested — a short GPT-4o query is estimated at ~0.43 WhSW9, but that is an average over datacentre throughput, not a statement about how much silicon was running during the answer. The instantaneous power to generate a response from a frontier model is, on public teardowns, measured in kilowatts, not watts. Anthropic's total datacentre capacity is reported at more than 1 GW, with inference estimated above 8% of that — more than 80 MW dedicated to keeping Claude answeringSW9. A drone in flight has a compute budget roughly one millionth of a single inference rack the model was trained to run on.

Power envelope: what flies vs what serves (log scale)

Cyan = flies comfortably within SWaP. Amber = fits only on Group 3 / larger platforms, or only with heavy compression. Red = datacentre serving, cannot fly regardless of compression trajectory. LLM serving numbers are GPU only; rack-level CPU, NVMe, networking and cooling typically add 40–80%. The visible gap

What this argument doesn't prove — the honest escape hatches

Non-negotiable caveats

The SWaP argument refutes the monolithic frontier model in the control loop. It does not refute the following, which are real, current, and form the operational edge stack KS actually builds on:

  • Quantised small models DO fly. Llama 3.x 1B–3B quantised to 4-bit hit 25–55 tokens/s on Jetson Orin Nano Super; Llama 3.2 3B is reported at 27.7 tokens/s on Jetson AI Lab's own numbersSW13. That means a small reasoning helper is genuinely usable on a Group 1 quadrotor — for operator chat, lightweight natural-language logging, voice callsigns. Not for flight-path generation.
  • Distilled specialist NNs fit comfortably. YOLO-class detection, mono-depth, visual SLAM, acoustic classifiers, optical-flow trackers all run at single-digit watts. This is the KS Tier 2 workload (§06). The SWaP argument is entirely consistent with the KS thesis — specialist NNs are exactly what the drone is supposed to carry.
  • Dedicated accelerators shift the curve but not the ceiling. Hailo-8 at 26 TOPS for 2.5 WSW11; Coral Edge TPU at 4 TOPS for 2 WSW10; Qualcomm and Intel mobile NPUs similar. They make specialist inference cheaper. They do not make a 70–400B monolithic policy runnable on a drone.
  • Compression research is moving, but not fast enough to matter here. 70B models compressed to 4-bit still need tens of GB of memory; frontier reasoning and long-context are the capabilities that resist compression most stubbornly. Even an optimistic 2027–2028 compression trajectory does not close three orders of magnitude.
  • Group 4–5 is a different argument. An MQ-9-class platform (>1,320 lb, generator-driven electrical bus, active cooling) can in principle carry datacentre-class silicon. The argument in this section is scoped to Group 1–3 — the attritable, expendable, tactical-edge tier that KS targets.

Why the gap still matters

The "Glass Brain" critique is about frontier-capability monolithic LLMs doing end-to-end autonomy — the seductive marketing pitch that a single 400B-parameter model, properly scaffolded, will fly the aircraft. Those models run in racks drawing kilowatts per request. Even with compression that preserves useful capability, they do not fit in 20 W. The KS federated architecture therefore routes frontier-class reasoning to the rear — HNS tablet, command-post compute, datacentre-sovereign model-host — and keeps only specialist NNs and the deterministic core on the airframe. SWaP physics forces the KS architecture; it is not a design preference.

This also frames the competitive honesty. Anduril's Ghost-X doing 32 TOPS onboardSW4 is an Orin-Nano-class number — specialist NN territory, not frontier LLM territory. Shield AI's Hivemind is a reinforcement-learned autonomy stack, not a frontier generative model. Every credible defence-autonomy vendor already lives inside the SWaP envelope described here. The Glass Brain is the direction hype pulls, not the direction shipping product goes.

One line, printed on the hangar wall. You cannot fly a Glass Brain — not metaphorically, literally. Frontier monolithic inference lives in racks drawing kilowatts per query. Tactical UAVs live in tens of watts. The federated architecture (§06) and the lego-block factorisation across platforms (§09) are what fits the airframe; everything else is a category error about where the silicon actually runs.

The companion paper, The Kill Chain v1.1, carries the same argument into the F2T2EA chain — in particular where the SWaP envelope forces the audit seam to live on the ground rather than on the airframe.

HNS — the dismounted soldier as a federation node

The human soldier is not "a user" of the KS brain — they are another node. They have sensors the federation wants (eyes, ears, trained judgement, a camera, a microphone, an IMU, a GPS, biometric telemetry). They need outputs the federation can curate (a HUD that shows the right things, quiet when it should be quiet, loud when it must be loud). Treat them the same way we treat any other node: a published micro-API contract, a Conscience around the AI-generated HUD content, a place on the priority ladder.

What HNS contributes

  • Trained judgement — the single best classifier on the battlefield for many ambiguous situations
  • ISR at ground truth — first-person EO/IR, acoustic, environmental
  • Voice-to-structured-report — natural language in, tagged observation out (LLM's one legitimate control-adjacent job)
  • Biometric telemetry — fatigue, stress, casualty indicators feeding predictive care
  • Confirmation gate — weapons release and sensitive actions can require HNS double-confirm

What HNS receives (Conscience-gated)

  • Curated SA — threats, friendlies, UAV/UGV feeds, priority alerts

  • Cognitive-load cap — the HUD Conscience refuses to show more than a human can process
  • Silent/haptic comms — critical info without breaking sound discipline

The difference from any non-human node: this one has legal authority, trained judgement, and the best general-purpose classifier evolution has produced. We would be mad not to put them on the bus.

10 · Micro-APIs

The Bezos Mandate for Autonomy.

"All teams will henceforth expose their data and functionality through service interfaces. Teams must communicate with each other through these interfaces. There will be no other form of inter-process communication allowed. Anyone who doesn't do this will be fired. Thank you; have a nice day!" — Jeff Bezos, internal mandate, Amazon, c.2002

That mandate turned Amazon from a bookstore into Amazon Web Services — now the largest cloud platform on earth. The mechanism was simple: every team's output is a public API contract. No shared databases. No private back-channels. No "we'll just import their module directly." Every capability becomes a service someone else can consume, compose, or replace.

Plain English — what we mean by "contract"

In software, a contract is a published, versioned promise between two parts of a system: "ask me for X in this format, and I will return Y in that format, within this time, and I will not crash on malformed input." It is a short specification — a schema or a document — that both sides agree on and neither can silently break. Exactly like a legal contract: the terms are explicit, enforceable, and if either side changes them the other is notified and given time to adapt. In practice this means a customer, a third-party module, or another drone on the swarm can plug into a KS service knowing exactly what they will receive and what they must supply — without ever reading a line of KS source code. Contracts are how Amazon became AWS, how the modern internet (HTTP, DNS, TLS) works at all, and how a supply chain of 100,000 suppliers builds a Boeing 787. It is the boring paperwork that makes very big systems possible.

KS applies the same discipline to the brain. Every section of the federated architecture is a micro-API. Every Tier 2 specialist. Every Tier 3 orchestrator function. Every Conscience check. Every comms channel. If it is a block in the diagram, it has a versioned, documented, testable contract.

Why The engineering payoff

  • Independent teams, independent release cadence
  • Block-level upgrades without system-wide retest
  • Field-replaceable modules — same contract, new vendor
  • Certification scope bounded per block

Why The strategic payoff

  • Third parties can build on the KS brain — ecosystem > empire
  • Customers can integrate their own payloads, sensors, comms
  • Open, auditable interfaces = regulator-friendly
  • A new platform (naval, loitering munition, sensor mast) consumes existing APIs on day one

Why The safety payoff

  • Every command has a traceable origin
  • Every block can be swapped for a deterministic mock in test
  • Contracts are the Conscience's evidence base
  • No hidden coupling = no hidden failure mode

11 · A Mission, Walked Through

The architecture in action — four scenes.

Abstract architecture arguments only go so far. Here is a single composite mission — a mixed-team ISR patrol — walked scene-by-scene so the federated brain, the Conscience, the priority ladder and the HNS node are visible doing their jobs. Every decision below is reproducible from the bus telemetry.

Setup

Operator tasks a mixed team: 1 × UAV (high-altitude ISR), 1 × UGV (ground patrol along sector boundary), 1 × HNS (rifleman with helmet cam, biometric, earpiece + HUD). Priority ladder armed: P1 Survive, P2 Exfil, P3 RTB, P4 Mission, P5 Opportunistic. EMCON mode BURST (radio silent, 1 ms TX windows only). All platforms run the same Tier 1 core, Tier 3 orchestrator and Conscience pattern; only the Tier 2 specialist mix differs.

Scene 1 — Nominal: a clean classification

T + 00:12:04. UAV perception (Tier 2) detects a vehicle track. Proposes: {class: T-72, conf: 0.94, bearing: 087°, dist: 2.1 km}.
Conscience gates: Physical ✓ · Envelope ✓ · Priority ✓ (P4 active, no higher override) · Temporal ✓ (track stable 8 s) · Confidence ✓ (0.94 > 0.85 threshold for ISR report).
Action: Tier 3 accepts. Track published to /tracks. Orchestrator pushes a HUD marker to HNS via hns.hud POST /marker — HUD Conscience rate-limits to 3 markers/min, accepts (current load: 0). HNS sees a single unobtrusive chevron at 087°. UGV receives the same track and updates its patrol pattern to keep the contact in frame.

Scene 2 — The AI tries something stupid (and is refused)

T + 00:14:31. Cognitive EW specialist detects unknown radar emitter, proposes: {action: active_jam, conf: 0.62}.
Conscience gates: Physical ✓ · Envelope ✓ · Priority ✗ FAIL (current EMCON is BURST — active jamming would violate the operator-set comms discipline which sits above any Tier 2 proposal).
Action: Proposal rejected. Fallback engaged: passive characterisation only, emitter logged, no transmission. Entry appended to conscience.rejects with full rationale. Operator will see it in the post-mission audit, but no unsafe action was taken. A Glass Brain with the same internal impulse would have had no second line.

Scene 3 — Adversarial spoof, multi-gate catch

T + 00:21:17. Enemy force projects a false thermal signature to draw the UGV into an ambush kill-box. UAV perception proposes: {class: high_value, conf: 0.78, bearing: 142°, dist: 900 m}.
Conscience gates: Physical ✓ · Envelope ✓ · Priority ✓ · Temporal ✗ FAIL (signature appears and disappears at ˜4 Hz — inconsistent with any real vehicle thermal profile). Cross-check: threat classifier reports pattern mismatch, independent confidence drops to 0.31.
Action: Proposal rejected on temporal-coherence. UGV continues planned patrol, stays out of kill-box. HNS HUD shows a small "ambiguous contact, rejected" chevron (informational, not actionable). The same adversarial input against a Glass Brain would have produced a single confident commitment with no second opinion.

Scene 4 — Uplink loss: graceful degradation, no drama

T + 00:28:02. Operator uplink drops (jamming on the command frequency). Comms module publishes {uplink: down, since: T+28:02}.
Tier 3 response (no AI consulted): priority ladder automatically shifts — P4 Mission continues under pre-declared limited unilateral authority, P3 RTB timer begins, P2 data exfil via last-gasp burst protocol if P1 Survive ever triggers. All platforms independently know this from their identical Tier 3 FSM.
HNS: earpiece emits a single haptic "comms down" pulse (silent to surroundings), HUD status bar ticks over to LUA mode, operator-curated tasks continue.
UAV / UGV: keep patrolling. No AI is asked what to do. No LLM is hallucinating a plan. The plan was in the code.

Scene 5 — Human-in-the-loop gate on weapons release

T + 00:33:41. Threat confirmed by two independent sensors (UAV perception 0.96, UGV acoustic 0.88). Orchestrator reaches an engagement decision point.
Conscience gate 5 for weapons release: requires explicit HNS authorisation via hns.auth POST /release with voice confirmation phrase — Conscience confidence gate for a weapons-class action is set to "require human".
Action: HNS speaks the phrase, voice LLM parses to structured confirmation, hns.auth returns signed token, Conscience passes, Tier 3 commits the action. Every step audited. Every step replayable. No monolithic model signed off on a kill. A human did — with the system's help, not instead of it.
Doctrine anchor — what gate 5 is satisfying

The operator interaction in Scene 5 is not a UX choice; it is the legal frame the architecture is engineered to satisfy. Article 36 of Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions requires every state to review new means and methods of warfare for compliance with international law. The companion concept — meaningful human control — is the standard increasingly cited in UK JSP 936, NATO doctrine and the EU AI Act for autonomous and AI-enabled weapons. Gate 5 is the engineering primitive that makes that control concrete: a signed human token, a deterministic refusal of any release without it, an immutable audit trail of who authorised what and when. The full Article 36 methodology, threshold calibration and degraded-comms behaviour belong in the Engagement & Cyber companion thesis (§16); the architectural foundation lives here.

What just happened

Five scenes, three platforms, one operator, one soldier, no LLM in any control path. Two AI proposals rejected before they could cause harm, one adversarial input caught, one uplink loss absorbed without anyone having to ask an AI for an opinion, one weapons release gated through a human. Every rejection, every acceptance, every state change written to an immutable audit log. A Glass Brain cannot produce this post-mission record because the decisions are not structured enough to record. A Federated Brain produces it as a side-effect of how it works.

12 · The Statistical Case

Directional numbers that support the argument.

Figures below are illustrative of direction and magnitude, drawn from published industry ranges. This is an internal document, not a peer-reviewed paper; a citations pass is scheduled before external release.

Compute cost per decision — log scale

A deterministic safety clamp costs effectively nothing. A specialist on-device NN is cheap. A frontier LLM is roughly six to eight orders of magnitude more expensive per decision. In a platform with 20 W of compute and zero uplink, this is not a preference — it is a constraint.

Failure impact: monolithic vs federated

A single monolithic brain failing means the platform failing. A federated brain losing one module degrades gracefully: the vehicle keeps flying, the orchestrator switches to a fallback, the mission priority ladder protects the "get the data home" outcome.

Industry trajectory: "pure LLM" share of shipped AI systems, 2022 → 2026

Even in consumer AI, "single model does everything" has been losing share every year to tool-use, agentic frameworks and deterministic scaffolding. The direction of travel is towards the federated architecture, not away from it.

Certification readiness by codebase class (directional)

DO-178C DAL-A and ISO 26262 ASIL-D are mature, priced and proven for deterministic code. Mixed-criticality (deterministic core + ML modules under one authority) sits on draft regulator guidance (EASA Concept Paper for Learning Assurance, RTCA activity) — real progress, but not yet a turnkey path. Monolithic neural policies at equivalent criticality have no accepted standard. KS is an early mover helping define the middle column, not a beneficiary of a priced path that already exists there.

The full regulatory-moat thesis — including how a Glass-Brain monolith fails Annex III at multiple articles simultaneously and why KS’s posture converts compliance cost into competitive advantage — is set out in the companion document KS — EU AI Act as Regulatory Moat v1.0.

15 · Objections & Rebuttals

The arguments against, answered.

"But one big model is the future."

Possibly, eventually. Not in a weapon system that must be certified, operated comms-silent, and survive an adversarial input today. We don't bet on the future being wrong — only on the present being real.

"Federated systems iterate slowly."

They iterate faster. Each block has a small blast radius. Retraining perception doesn't break SLAM. 41 KS revisions in 4 weeks is evidence, not theory.

"You'll miss emergent behaviour."

We specifically don't want emergent behaviour in the control path. Emergence is great in chat. Emergence is a flight incident in control.

"The Conscience will be the bottleneck."

The Conscience is five deterministic checks. Microseconds per decision — the same order as a CAN-bus frame. The bottleneck is never the guard rail. It is the thing the guard rail is protecting you from.

"End-to-end learned policies will outperform engineered stacks."

In narrow benchmarks, sometimes. In shipping autonomy with a safety bar, not yet. Waymo > Tesla FSD on every real-world reliability metric that has been measured. Until that reverses, we ship the architecture that clears the bar today.

"This is classical engineering with ML bolted on."

Correct — and that is the argument. Classical engineering has a 100-year track record of not killing people. ML has a 10-year record of surprising its authors.

16 · Out of Scope — Engagement & Cyber Thesis

What this doc deliberately does not solve.

Two substantial bodies of work are deliberately left out of this thesis:

  • Engagement doctrine — Rules of Engagement (ROE), IFF (Identification Friend or Foe), kill-chain authority, positive target identification (PID), deconfliction (air / ground / electromagnetic), Article 36 weapons reviews, meaningful human control under LOAC, targeting under uncertainty, coalition interoperability.
  • Cyber, sovereignty & resilience — attack surface analysis, prompt-injection defence, adversarial-ML poisoning, supply-chain integrity of model weights and training data, UK/EU-sovereign compute and model stack, secure boot / firmware integrity, red-team methodology, incident response, forensic replay, cross-coalition information assurance.

Both deserve their own deep treatment, by people qualified to write them, with the right reviewers in the room. Both are the subject of a companion document (working title: Meaningful Control & Resilience — The Engagement and Cyber Thesis), drafted separately.

Foundations laid in this doc

  • Tier 1 deterministic core — immutable safety envelope and a minimal attack surface. Smaller, auditable, MISRA-clean code is the easiest thing in the system to harden.
  • Conscience five-gate pattern — directly applicable to both engagement and adversarial-ML defence. Temporal-coherence (Gate 4) catches many spoof attacks as a side effect; Gate 5 blocks low-confidence weapons-class actions.
  • Priority ladder with SURVIVE at P1 — no engagement action can override self-preservation; no opportunistic AI proposal can override the operator-set mission state.
  • HNS authorisation endpoint (hns.auth POST /release) — one human, one signed token, one auditable action. Software foundation for chain-of-command authority.
  • Audit log / telemetry-always-on — every proposal, rejection, authorisation and state transition replayable post-mission. Basis for Article 36 compliance and cyber incident forensics.
  • Micro-API contracts — integration surface for IFF feeds, BFT tracks, deconfliction services, weapons-authority tokens — and the natural boundary for zero-trust auth, mutual TLS, token scoping.
  • PQC + DDS comms — Kyber/Dilithium hybrid already live in the comms layer. Post-quantum foundation ready for the cyber companion to build on.
  • Small, sovereign, on-device — EMCON-survivable stack runs without cloud dependency; no US-hosted model weights in the control path to sanction-risk.

To be covered in the companion doc

  • Engagement: IFF integration (Mode 5, BFT, UAS-C2 auth); PID doctrine and classifier calibration; deconfliction air/ground/electromagnetic; Article 36 methodology; LOAC under degraded comms; coalition interoperability (NATO STANAGs); Gate-5 threshold calibration authority; meaningful-human-control under latency and at edge of reach; escalation, abort, recall under contested comms; allied-asset deconfliction.
  • Cyber: threat model for each tier and interface; prompt-injection defence for the Tier 4 LLM; adversarial-ML robustness for Tier 2 specialists (input perturbations, model inversion, membership inference); supply-chain attestation for model weights and training data; firmware / secure boot chain; zero-trust between micro-APIs; red-team playbook; incident-response runbooks; cross-coalition information assurance; classified-data handling across the bus.
  • Sovereignty: UK/EU-native compute and model provenance; no-US-dependency variant of the stack; ITAR / dual-use export-control posture; alternative key-management under non-US sovereignty.
Why call this out explicitly. A thesis that argues for a federated brain, a Conscience, a priority ladder and PQC comms can appear to have solved engagement and cyber because it names many of the right primitives. It hasn't. The architectural foundations in §§06–10 were designed to carry that weight. The doctrine and threat modelling must be worked — with the right legal, ethical, operational, regulatory and cyber inputs — before weight is applied. This thesis intentionally stops at the edge of engagement and at the surface of the cyber threat model.
17 · Conclusion

Break the Glass Brain. Build a federation.

The Glass Brain is already cracking under its own weight. Even the labs that build the biggest models are quietly wrapping them in tool-use, critics, orchestrators and constitutions. The autonomous-vehicle industry — the one domain where AI has faced physics and regulators — converged on federation a decade ago. Commercial aviation never left.

Karneth Strata's brain is a federation. Four tiers. A deterministic core that cannot be overridden. Specialist AI modules where pattern-recognition genuinely wins. A mission orchestrator that owns the priority ladder. An optional LLM interface at the edges for operators. Around every AI module, the AI Conscience — five hard gates that make sure the model's proposals are sane before they become commands. Every block a published micro-API contract in the Bezos tradition. Every platform — UAV, UGV, PiDog, and the dismounted soldier as HNS — assembled from the same lego blocks.

We love AI. We just won't use it to fly the aircraft. The result is a brain that is designed to be certifiable, explainable, resilient, upgradeable, cheap to run, survivable in EMCON, common across every KS platform, ecosystem-friendly, first-to-revenue, and honest about what AI is actually good for. Against a monolithic competitor the combination is decisive; within the federated camp shared with Anduril, Waymo and Skydio, the defensible KS claim is a smarter composition, deeper safety-critical credentials, UK/EU sovereignty, and faster iteration at smaller scale — not displacement of incumbents with 100× the resources.
