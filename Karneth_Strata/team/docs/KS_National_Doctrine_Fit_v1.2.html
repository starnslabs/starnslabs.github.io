Section 1

§1Purpose & scope

Two primary-source national doctrine documents were added to the KS reference corpus on 2026-04-24. Both are dated within the last seven months and represent the current political framing of the two largest contributors to NATO conventional defence: the United States and Germany. This document summarises each, compares them against each other, and scorecards Karneth Strata's current doctrine corpus against both. The intent is to give the KS team a DD-ready view of where KS sits inside the 2026 doctrinal landscape — what aligns, what doesn't, and where the pitch material needs sharpening.

Section 2 · United States

§2US NDS 2026 — summary

Framing. "Peace through strength, America First." The doctrine explicitly breaks with post–Cold War grand strategy and with nation-building. It embraces what it calls the "warrior ethos" and a "flexible realism" approach: evaluate, sort, prioritise. The threats Americans face are sorted rather than all treated equally, and allies are expected to carry more of the load for their own regions.

The four Lines of Effort (LoEs)

LoE 1 · Defend the US Homeland

Border security, counter-narco-terrorism (now FTOs), Golden Dome missile shield, counter-UAS as a named top-line priority, nuclear modernisation, cyber defence, scoped counter-terrorism. Explicit "Trump Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine" guaranteeing US access to Greenland, the Panama Canal, the "Gulf of America."

LoE 2 · Deter China in the Indo-Pacific

Denial defence along the First Island Chain (FIC), not dominance. "Prevent anyone from dominating us or our allies." Open PLA mil-to-mil communications for de-escalation. Indo-Pacific = "world's largest market area" → "half of global economy" → strategic imperative.

LoE 3 · Burden-share with allies and partners

Hague Summit standard: 5% of GDP total defence spend (3.5% hard-mil + 1.5% security-related), pushed globally not just in Europe. Europe takes primary responsibility for conventional defence vs Russia; US plays a more limited supporting role. South Korea leads vs DPRK. Israel + Gulf partners lead vs Iran.

LoE 4 · Supercharge the US DIB

Re-shore defence industry. Adopt AI. Build at scale for US + allies. Clear regulations. Explicitly described as "national mobilisation on par with WWII / Cold War revivals."

Implicit doctrinal stances

  • Attritable autonomy acceptable — Replicator-style posture. Platform loss is policy-tolerable if the mission effect is delivered.
  • Resource-sustainable counter-terror — scoped, not open-ended nation-building.
  • Simultaneity problem — the US cannot fight two major-theatre wars unassisted; alliance burden-sharing is the answer.
Section 3 · Germany

§3DE Bundeswehr 2026 — summary (translated from German)

Framing. First-ever combined Bundeswehr Military Strategy + Capability Profile, signed April 2026. The public excerpt runs 36 pages; the full version is GEHEIM-classified. Minister Pistorius's stated goal: "Germany goes first — pacemaker among European nations." The doctrine is unambiguous: Germany will become Europe's strongest conventional army by 2039 and will step into a new leadership role inside NATO.

Translation note. Key German terms carry specific doctrinal meaning and don't fully render in English. They're kept in German below with the translated sense in brackets. Full glossary at §8.

The 3-phase growth plan

Phase 1 — to 2029

Fokussierter Aufwuchs [focused build-up]. Maximise current readiness and sustainment. Use existing resources harder.

Phase 2 — to 2035

Strukturierter Aufwuchs [structured build-up]. Durable force aligned to NATO defence plans. Target personnel: 460,000 active + non-active by 2035.

Phase 3 — to 2039+

Tech-superior force. "Strongest conventional army in Europe." Locks Germany's European leadership role.

Kriegsbild — the five-pillar "war picture"

The Kriegsbild is the Bundeswehr's explicit characterisation of modern conflict and is the load-bearing doctrinal assumption for every capability decision below it.

1. Entgrenzung des Krieges [Erasure of boundaries]

Civil/military, home/battlefield, war/peace, combatant/non-combatant — all deliberately blurred by the adversary. No reliance on ethical or legal norms being respected. German society as a whole is a target.

2. Multi-temporal war

Legacy + modern + next-gen weapons run simultaneously. Quantum computing and robotics alongside COTS expendables alongside conventional fires. Innovation + adaptation speed decide outcomes.

3. Transparent battlefield

Data is a weapon. AI augments cognition. Real-time ISR is ambient — hiding is hard. Information superiority is fought for in time/space windows and converts into command and effect superiority.

4. Wirkung auf Abstand [Stand-off effect]

Precision effects across all ranges. No safe rear areas. Survival and operational tempo require being able to reach deep into the adversary's space.

5. Effiziente Masse [Efficient mass]

Cheap mass-produced systems paired with high-value assets. Quantity becomes its own quality. "Economy of war" matters again — sustaining the mix over time is the win condition.

Strategic role — Germany's new shape

Anlehnungspartner

"Framework / backbone partner." Germany becomes the anchor smaller European allies lean on. First permanent overseas brigade: Lithuania. "NATO must become more European to stay transatlantic."

One-Theatre-Approach

Globally holistic, dimension-spanning. A regional event must be checked for coupling with other geostrategic spaces. Multi-domain operations are the default, not the exception.

Six National Capability Goals

#Capability GoalCore claim
aDeep Precision Strike + integrated air & missile defenceLong-range precision fires out to theatre depth; all-range AD against long-range weapons and carriers
bInformation SuperiorityWin and hold info dominance via AI, in all dimensions, offensive and defensive
cMDO-Enabled Alliance / Digital BackboneCloud IT platform + resilient/redundant comms, pursuing Digitale Souveränität (digital sovereignty)
dNational command for MDO + Deep Precision StrikeSovereign operational-level command capability, including cross-domain fire
eOperationsbasis DeutschlandSovereign home-territory operating base: airspace, territorial waters, data and comms networks
fNational crisis & risk responseWhole-of-government capability; protection of German nationals abroad

Innovation imperative

  • Bottom-up from the troops — not just from procurement HQ.
  • Tight civil-industry integration — military, research, and national civilian economy "closely interlocked."
  • Replace legacy earlier than normal lifecycle — where existing systems can be replaced at lower cost with faster effect, do it from Phase 1.
  • Quantity becomes quality — cheap mass-produced effectors as a pillar, not an afterthought.
Section 4 · Comparison

§4Side-by-side — where the two align, where they diverge

The two doctrines are explicitly coupled. The German doc names the US NDS directly in §5.2: "the USA demand greater efforts from their allies to guarantee their own security. Germany must therefore become an even stronger military ally of the United States and take more responsibility together with its allies for common euro-atlantic security." The US NDS in turn names Europe (led by Germany's economy specifically) as capable of taking primary conventional defence responsibility against Russia.

Delivery mechanism for the sovereignty rows — the lead-box methodology

Two of the scorecard rows above — European sovereignty / regulatory moat and Operationsbasis / national command sovereignty — sit at Moderate gap precisely because they are doctrinally demanded but engineering-thin in most vendors' answers. The US NDS 2026 calls for resilient, allied-controllable AI supply chains; the Bundeswehr Gesamtkonzeption names Digitale Souveränität and end-to-end controllability as preconditions for autonomous-system procurement. Both doctrines therefore require a concrete delivery mechanism, not a posture statement.

KS's answer is the lead-box methodology: a sovereignty-native, locally-hosted compute environment in which models, weights, prompts, source code, and customer data never leave the box. It is a primary architectural choice rather than a retrofit, which is the distinction that survives a sceptical procurement audit. Full specification — hardware reference architecture, phase-keyed adoption, software stack, and procurement plan — is in Assets/Docs/Internal/KS_Lead_Box_Methodology_v1.0.html and Assets/Docs/Internal/KS_AI_Tooling_Policy_v1.0.html. The relevance here is narrower: the lead-box methodology is the named engineering-side delivery for the sovereignty alignment claims this document makes against both NDS 2026 and the Bundeswehr Gesamtkonzeption.

Section 6 · Gaps

§6Wide gaps — the critical five

Only the gaps rated Critical or High are listed here. Low / Moderate gaps are addressed in §7 but are not load-bearing for DD.

1. Naval / maritime denial · High · funded future

Status v1.1: gap is now a named funded-future initiative. Phase 4 — Maritime Platform Extension (USV/UUV) added to KS_Roadmap_2026_2030 v1.4 as a post-programme initiative contingent on Series-A / follow-on funding. Paired 7th pillar in KS_Future_Tech v1.1. Architectural rationale: the KS federated C2 + Conscience stack is platform-agnostic; maritime extension is a thin adapter layer (COLREGS constraints, AIS fusion, maritime comms stack, saltwater-rated BOM, naval-domain advisor hire for integration signoff). Scope-honest: not shipped, not on 2026-2030 programme budget, but is now on the roadmap rather than silently absent.

2. Strategic depth / Deep Precision Strike · Critical

KS operates at km-scale swarm range. Both doctrines demand 100+ km stand-off. KS is not pitching as strike; it's a tactical C2 + autonomy layer under strike systems. The doctrine docs need this stated explicitly so reviewers don't read KS as over-claiming.

3. Hybrid warfare / Entgrenzung / homefront · High

Completely absent from the KS corpus. Bundeswehr pillar #1 of Kriegsbild. Needs either a new companion doc or a Glass-Brain section. Also touches the deferred Engagement & Cyber thesis.

4. DIB / supply-chain sovereignty · High

KS BOM is global COTS (Nvidia Jetson = TSMC). US NDS demands re-shoring; DE demands Digitale Souveränität. Future_Tech needs a sovereignty column showing which techs survive a Taiwan-contingency and which don't.

5. Explicit Russia threat model · High

Glass_Brain has a European-sovereignty angle but no Russia-specific doctrine characterisation (hybrid, decoupling intent, Baltic threat, undersea / space / cyber reach). DE doctrine puts Russia at #1 — KS can't remain neutral on the characterisation.

Bonus · 5% GDP Hague tailwind · High

Commercially this is a pure win that KS isn't exploiting. 3.5% hard-mil → tier-2 sub-prime spend. 1.5% security-related → homeland C-UAS + CNI resilience adjacency. Needs one page in Commercial_Applications.

The uncomfortable one. The attritable-autonomy tension is real but interpretive. US doctrine embraces acceptable loss; KS materials emphasise survivability (last-gasp, self-destruct, hide-and-seek). KS's architecture does support mission-abort and expendable postures, but the pitch language reads survival-first. A DD reviewer from the US side may read this as misaligned with Replicator doctrine. Soft fix: be explicit in Kill_Chain §9 that the survival stack is mission-configurable and includes a "mission over platform" mode.
