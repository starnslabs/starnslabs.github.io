§2 · What we do

2In plain language

Karneth Strata is a software platform that sits between human operators and unmanned systems. It gives a small team the ability to command many autonomous or semi-autonomous drones, ground vehicles, and maritime platforms from a single operator station — with full audit trail, human-in-the-loop governance, and safe-behaviour guarantees.

In practical terms, a KS-equipped operator can:

  • Direct a mixed fleet of air, ground, and (in roadmap) maritime platforms from one screen
  • Assign missions to the fleet and let the platforms execute them autonomously, with the operator intervening by exception
  • Maintain command of the mission even when communications are partially denied or jammed
  • Produce a complete, inspectable record of every decision the system made — for accountability, after-action review, and regulatory compliance

KS does not build the drones, ground vehicles, or hulls themselves. We are the brain layer — the software and decision architecture that makes platforms from multiple suppliers work as a coherent operator-commanded force. That is a deliberate choice: it lets allied nations choose their own hardware suppliers while using a common command layer, which is exactly the direction European defence procurement is moving.

That choice opens two deployment shapes:

  • New fleet. Customers specifying new unmanned systems choose any hardware supplier and run KS on top. The command layer is sovereign from day one.
  • Existing fleet — sovereign retrofit. Customers with drones, ground vehicles, or maritime platforms already in service can replace the brain while keeping the airframe. Many of those platforms were built in supply chains that are no longer politically acceptable. Re-braining turns them into fleet the operator can audit, control, and use — at a fraction of the cost and political complexity of procurement replacement.

Retrofit is a second, adjacent market. Defence procurement leaders have been asking for it. Most command-software vendors cannot offer it because they are locked to their own platforms.

The one-line positioning: a small European team builds the operator-level command software for autonomous systems, with the safety and audit discipline that defence procurement demands and the platform-agnosticism that European alliance-building requires — whether the hardware is new or already in service.
§3 · Why it matters

3The capability gap we close

The current market shape is this:

The supply side

Dozens of European drone, ground-vehicle, and maritime-platform manufacturers, each building their own hardware and their own proprietary command software. The software is usually tied to a single vendor's platforms.

The buyer side

Ministries of defence, allied commands, and increasingly private-sector operators who need to combine platforms from many suppliers into one mission — and cannot, because each vendor's software does not talk to the others.

The current options for the buyer are unattractive: either lock in to a single vendor's stack (loses supplier competition, loses sovereignty), or hand-integrate every combination (doesn't scale, doesn't certify). Neither path fits a 5-per-cent-GDP European build-up.

KS provides the third path: a vendor-independent command layer that absorbs platforms from any supplier who will integrate. That is architecturally simple to say and operationally hard to build — which is exactly why the moat is real, and why an experienced team matters more than the language of the pitch.

§5 · Current state

5What exists today

Karneth Strata is past the whiteboard stage. A working simulation platform runs today, capable of coordinating mixed air and ground unmanned systems in realistic mission scenarios. Hardware-in-the-loop integration has been validated against multiple platform classes. A live integration with robotic hardware is operational for demonstration to visiting partners and potential customers.

The company's current technical depth is documented in an extensive internal corpus covering architecture, decision methodology, cyber resilience, operational concepts, and commercial applications. That material is calibrated for specialist technical reviewers and is not reproduced in this brief — by design. See §9 for how it is made available.

Engineering posture — the Lead-Box methodology

KS engineering operates in a lead-box methodology — a locally-hosted compute environment in which models, weights, prompts, source code, mission data, and customer payload data never leave the box. Sovereignty-native by design, not retrofitted. The architecture removes a known procurement blocker for defence and regulated buyers from day one: UK MoD, US ITAR, NATO equivalents, and most primes' subcontract terms forbid customer-touching code from being processed by US-hosted AI APIs, and most AI-augmented startups cannot bid on this work as a result. KS can.

Capital implication: the methodology is procured in phases, with a Phase-1 commitment under £10K and a fully team-scaled cluster at ~£35-40K. Cost of AI-assisted engineering trends to electricity-only after the capital spend, rather than scaling with team size against a cloud API. Detail in the Lead-Box Methodology v1.0 companion document, available at the NDA-gated tier (see §9).

Stage summary in one sentence: working demonstrator, early pilot customer conversations in progress, seeking Series-A to move from demonstrator to first-customer deployment and to scale the team.

§8 · Honest risks

8What could go wrong

The honest-caveat discipline the team applies to engineering also applies to this brief. Every early-stage company has real risks; naming them is a sign of seriousness, not weakness.

Capital risk

KS is pre-revenue. Series-A shortfall, or a longer-than-planned gap between rounds, would slow hiring and delay certification. Mitigation: a tight build-burn plan with explicit quarterly gate criteria — the company does not spend ahead of milestones.

Team-scaling risk

The current core team is small and senior. Bringing in mid-level engineers fast enough to absorb Series-A capital is its own execution problem. Mitigation: a deliberate mentoring model with senior engineers carrying early hires through ramp.

Procurement-timing risk

Defence procurement cycles are long. Even with a strong product, a first contract can take eighteen to thirty-six months from first meeting to signed order. Mitigation: a pipeline of simultaneous early conversations rather than dependence on any single customer, plus a dual-use civilian pillar to de-risk pure defence timing.

Regulatory risk

The European AI Act, existing arms-export controls, and the emerging regulatory frameworks for autonomous systems will evolve during the company's growth window. Mitigation: the safety-and-compliance discipline is designed in from the start rather than retrofitted, which is a moat for KS but would be a risk if the posture were reversed.

What is not on this list, and not hidden: we do not believe the core technical risk is novel. The engineering problem is hard but understood. The risks above are commercial and operational, which are the ones investors are actually equipped to help with.
