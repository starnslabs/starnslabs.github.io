The Tank That Wouldn't Die

A Swarm Destroy story, told in the order the operator saw it · v1.0 2026-05-09 · v1.1 2026-05-11
Listen · narrated audiobook
Kokoro bm_lewis (British male) · built from the same text as the page below · v1.1 narration 2026-05-11

It is just past dusk over the Sachseln valley. Eight quads hold a lawnmower pattern at three thousand metres above ground level, beating north–south across a gravel pit and a stand of conifers, sensors pointed straight down. None of them is talking. Out at the C2 console, the operator watches the Mission Timeline tick along — a row per agent, time flowing left to right, nothing yet to mark.

This is a Swarm Destroy mission. The doctrine has three roles. Swarm 3 is recon: it sees, it decides what is interesting, it tells the rest of the swarm. Swarm 2 is the corroborator and the assessor: it goes and looks closer, vouches for what Swarm 3 saw, and after the strike it comes back to tell the operator what is left. Swarm 1 is the striker: it carries the warhead, locks on, and finishes the engagement when authorised.

The operator is not part of the swarm. The operator is the conscience. Nothing kinetic happens without their finger on the dialog.

Act I — A glint in the gravel

UAV-04, lawnmowering on the third leg, sees something it does not like. Bottom of frame, 0.7 seconds, classified as a probable tracked vehicle. It does not radio anything yet. Recon deduplicates contacts spatially — two sightings of the same target within a couple of hundred metres are the same contact, not a duplicate. Separately, when a known contact is still being seen, recon throttles its wire-publish heartbeat to a configurable cadence (default: thirty seconds, or five metres of movement, whichever comes first). It does not want to drown the C2 link in chatter.

Two seconds later UAV-04 sees the same shape from a slightly different aspect, agrees with itself, and pushes the contact onto the swarm bus. On the operator's timeline, a small cyan tick appears in UAV-04's row.

RECONT+00:14UAV-04 publishes SwarmContact at grid 6320, 4480 — class “tracked”, confidence 0.62.

The bus message is a broadcast. Every Swarm 2 agent on the network hears it. Each one quietly does the same arithmetic: am I idle, am I closest, am I free to go? Eight Swarm 2 quads compute their own sqrMagnitude to the contact and wait. The tie is broken by lowest agent ID. UAV-09 wins. The other seven stand down, their _busyInvestigators set unchanged.

Closest idle wins. One tank, one investigator. If recon publishes a second contact later, the next idle Swarm 2 takes it. — Phase 2 discipline, 2026-05-07
INVESTT+00:15UAV-09 self-tasks. Picks a random bearing on the disc, scales to 300 m stand-off, projects to XZ, resolves altitude as terrain + 600 m AGL. Cancels its boot patrol, accepts the new waypoint task.

The light-blue INVEST bar appears in UAV-09's lane. The other agents keep mowing.

Act II — Two pairs of eyes

The corroboration rule is simple and old: nothing is real until two of you say so independently. UAV-09 arrives at its stand-off and points its down-look sensor at the published position. Its onboard classifier agrees with UAV-04. SwarmContact count goes from one to two. The threshold trips.

The instant the threshold trips, two things happen at once.

The CORR thumbs populate. Each corroborator's down-look sensor cam takes a frame and pushes it into a per-corroborator side cache. The blackboard inspector now has two thumbnails, each labelled with the UAV that took it. CORR1 is UAV-04's frame; CORR2 is UAV-09's. The operator clicks one — it expands. There is a tank in the gravel. Tracks visible, hull pointing roughly north, no infantry around it.

CONTACTSC-0014
CLASSTRACKED, hostile
CORROBORATIONS2 / 2
CORR1 / CORR2UAV-04 / UAV-09
CONFIDENCE0.84

The fire request goes up. UAV-09, as the agent that crossed the line, publishes a FireAuthRequest back to C2. The dialog appears — globally mounted now, so it lands on whichever tab the operator happens to be viewing, and is draggable so it does not pin them to one screen. Title strip, state banner, fields stacked on separate rows so nothing collides with the bottom border.

CORRT+01:02UAV-09 down-look agrees. CORR1/CORR2 thumbs cache. Threshold tripped.
FIRE‑REQT+01:02Dialog mounts on DisplaySwitcher_Canvas. Operator sees it without changing tab.

Act III — Lock and look

Meanwhile UAV-04, the original recon, has switched modes. On first contact it broke its lawnmower legs and entered orbit lock-on: twelve waypoints around the contact, 300 m radius, 1000 m AGL, looped. The yellow LOCK-ON bar lights up in its lane on the timeline. From the operator's perspective the recon is now staring at the target while everything else gets organised.

The strikers — Swarm 1 — are in transit. Three quads detach from their holding pattern and burn south. The Quadcopter envelope at boot is now force-set to 83 m/s (about 300 kph) with 25 m/s² acceleration and 240°/s turn rate. Older prefabs serialised stale 30/4/120 numbers; Greg's Start() override on the controller makes sure every airframe is FPV-class regardless of what the prefab thinks. The strikers cover the kilometre in seconds.

Act IV — The decision

The dialog has three states the operator cares about. Pending shows the contact and its corroborators, the recommended strikers, and an editable list of which UAVs will go in. Granted shows that the operator pressed APPROVE and the strike is in flight. AuthComplete shows the closeout and waits for the operator to press CLOSE.

Auto-dismiss used to do the closeout. Auto-dismiss is gone. Terminal states require an explicit acknowledgement: the operator has to press CLOSE and confirm they have read the result. This is one of those decisions that costs nothing on the happy path and saves you on the day a strike outcome scrolls off-screen unread.

The operator reads the dialog, glances at the CORR1 thumb one more time, and presses APPROVE.

GRANTEDT+01:28Operator approves. FireAuthGrant on the wire. Three strikers commit.

Act V — Detonations and the per-UAV truth

Five strikers go in. The dialog now shows, in a two-line wrapped strip, exactly what each one did:

STRIKERSUAV0:HIT   UAV1:HIT   UAV2:SD   UAV3:HIT   UAV4:HIT

HIT means the warhead reached the target volume and detonated. SD means “stand-down” — the striker's own logic decided not to detonate. Maybe the LOS was wrong at the last moment, maybe the target had moved out of the strike envelope, maybe the airframe was already obsolete to the engagement because four others had already struck. Whatever the reason, it stood itself down. FireAuthRecord.StrikesStoodDown ticks.

The point is that the operator does not have to interpret a single “strike succeeded / failed” verdict. They see five rows, five outcomes, and the swarm's own honest count of how it went.

HIT × 4T+01:42Four warheads detonate. One striker stands down voluntarily.

Act VI — BDA and the closeout

One Swarm 2 quad has been waiting for the smoke to clear, twelve hundred metres back at altitude. Its job is Battle Damage Assessment: go and look, decide what is left. The light-blue BDA RTE bar appears in its lane the moment it accepts the task and points its nose at the impact point.

The BDA agent now has a nuance the older code did not. If it is high above the impact (more than 500 m above) it bypasses the line-of-sight check that would otherwise reject the assessment angle — at altitude the camera has the whole crater anyway. It also resolves its standoff as terrain + AGL rather than a fixed world Y, so it parks 600 m above the wreck regardless of valley elevation.

The BDA agent arrives, takes its pictures, and publishes BDA Receipt. This receipt is the canonical close-out signal. It used to be that the kill chain only closed when every striker had been individually dispositioned, which sometimes never converged — a stand-down agent in flight could leave the chain open indefinitely. Now: BDA in, chain closes. If BDA times out without a receipt, the chain still closes, with outcome Missed, so the operator always gets a CLOSE button to press.

BDAT+02:09UAV-11 receipt. Outcome: Destroyed. Kill tally: 1.
CLOSET+02:14Operator acknowledges. Dialog dismisses.

The blackboard inspector now shows the full record on a single row, two lines per fire-auth: identity and state on top, hits / stand-downs / BDA result and age below. hits=4/5 [0,1,3,4] sd=1 [2] bda=Destroyed. The operator can scroll back through every authorisation they have ever signed for, in this scene, for as long as the scene runs.

Act VII — A second cycle, mid-engagement

Between the operator pressing APPROVE and the warheads actually arriving, there is a window of forty to ninety seconds. The strikers are inbound but the tank is still standing. The dialog shows GRANTED. The timeline shows orange and green bars. Nothing yet has happened to the target.

In that window, a second light-blue INVEST bar appears in a different Swarm 2 lane. A peer of UAV-09 is self-tasking to a stand-off. Around the same target.

This is not a bug. It is the swarm doing exactly what its doctrine says.

Swarm 1 recon does not stop mowing because the kill chain is mid-flight. It does not know about fire authorisations, strike orders, or BDA. Its job is one job: continuously search, classify, and publish anything that looks worth publishing. While the strikers are in transit, UAV-04 — or another recon airframe on its lawnmower — sweeps back over the same gravel pit. The tank is still there. The classifier still agrees. The original SwarmContact may have aged out of the proximity-dedupe window, or the recon airframe is a different one that hasn't seen this target before. Either way, a fresh contact goes on the bus.

Every idle Swarm 2 does the arithmetic. The closest one accepts the task. Light-blue bar lights up.

Recon doesn't blink. While the kill chain runs, recon keeps verifying — and the swarm keeps a second pair of eyes routing to the target, in case the strike misses or the tank is something the strikers can't kill. By the time the second investigator arrives, either the wreck is on fire and the engagement is closing out, or it isn't, and we have a fresh sensor in position to call for round two. — Doctrine, observed live 2026-05-09 evening

This is concurrency the way a real swarm is supposed to do it. Recon and strike run as independent loops. The kill chain is one finite-state machine; recon's continuous-classify is another. They share a bus, not a master clock. A second INVEST during the strike window is the visible signature of that independence, not a leak between caches.

The only place this could be tightened is in the operator's view. A future polish: when a contact is already in FireAuthGranted state and a new contact lands within proximity of the existing one, render the new INVEST marker dim or in a paler shade so the operator's eye reads “this is the same tank, second-pass cycle” rather than “new threat detected.” The behaviour stays correct; the operator just gets a calmer timeline.

Epilogue — The tank that almost wasn't visible

Before any of the above could happen, the tank had to actually appear in the CORR thumbs. For most of the day, it didn't.

The CORR thumb pipeline takes a one-frame snapshot from the corroborator's down-look cam, the moment the threshold trips. The first version of this rendered an empty grey frame. The second version rendered ground. The third rendered ground and a very small dot. It took six iterations to actually see the tank.

ITER 1Render blank — corroborator's onboard cam disabled. Fall back to vehicle.OnboardCamera.
ITER 2Tank too small. Push the cam close to the contact for capture, ~55 m above-and-behind, pop back after.
ITER 3Cam aims at transform.position — but the tank's prefab pivot is at ground level. Aim at Renderer.bounds.center instead.
ITER 4Tank still missing. The mesh is on a child named Tank which is activeInHierarchy=False in tac-map mode. Recursively wake inactive descendants for the render, restore in finally.
ITER 5Tank is there but blurry — LOD distance cull. Force QualitySettings.lodBias=100 for the render duration.
ITER 6Sphere markers from the tac-map dome occlude the model. Hide SpheresRoot during render. Tank visible.

Six bugs, one frame. Each one perfectly reasonable in isolation. Together: a frame that looked grey for the entire day until the last one got fixed.

Technical appendix — for the software engineer

What follows is the same story re-told as code structure. Strip the tank-and-operator framing and what you have is a distributed event-driven system with a single coordinator, a soft-real-time scheduler per agent, and a few caches whose lifetimes determine the system's behaviour at the seams.

Architectural pieces

SwarmBlackboard — process-singleton, holds per-agent snapshots (position, mission state, sensor packet). Updated by each agent on its own tick. Read by anyone who needs a consistent view.

KillChainCoordinator — singleton MonoBehaviour exposing C# events: OnContactReceived, OnContactCorroborated, OnFireAuthRequested, OnFireAuthGranted, OnFireAuthDenied, OnBdaReceipt, etc. Owns the active FireAuthRecord dictionary keyed by request-id.

Swarm{1,2,3}xxxBehaviour — per-agent MonoBehaviours attached at boot by MissionPlannerSubmitter based on SwarmIdentity. Each is a small state machine: Idle → ToStandoff → Investigating → Corroborated → RequestedFireAuth, etc.

SwarmContact — DTO on the bus, gets a 120 s TTL in the recon cache. Dedupe is by spatial proximity within TTL window.

FireAuthRecord — server-side mirror of the dialog. Tracks pending strikers, per-UAV outcomes (Hit, StoodDown), BdaResult, lifecycle state.

MissionTimelineTab — UI tab. Subscribes to the audit firehose and turns each event into a coloured fixed-duration bar in the appropriate agent's row.

The closest-idle election

When recon publishes a contact, every Swarm2InvestigateBehaviour on every Swarm 2 agent runs the same election — locally, with no coordinator round-trip. The pseudocode:

HandleContactReceived(msg):
    if _state != Idle: return                  // already busy, leave it alone
    if not IsClosestIdleSwarm2(msg.Position):  // someone else is closer
        return
    BeginInvestigation(msg.ContactId, msg.Position)

IsClosestIdleSwarm2(pos):
    best = (id=-1, distSq=∞)
    for id in SwarmIdentity.GetAgentsInSwarm(2):
        if id in _busyInvestigators: continue          // exclude in-flight peers
        snap = blackboard.TryGetAgentSnapshot(id)
        d = (snap.Position - pos).sqrMagnitude
        if d < best.distSq or (d == best.distSq and id < best.id):
            best = (id, d)
    return best.id == _agentId

This is gossip-style consensus without a vote: every agent reaches the same conclusion deterministically because they read the same blackboard and use the same tiebreak. The static _busyInvestigators set is the only shared mutable state, and it only grows on a successful AssignTask and shrinks on ResetToIdle. Zero coordinator round-trips per contact.

The CORR thumb pipeline

When the corroboration threshold trips, the coordinator fires OnContactCorroborated. Swarm2BdaBehaviour (which doubles as the side-cache owner) listens, takes a one-frame snapshot from the corroborator's down-look Camera, blits it into a RenderTexture, copies into a Texture2D in the side cache keyed by (contactId, corroboratorAgentId). The blackboard inspector reads from this cache when populating CORR1 / CORR2 RawImages.

Three Unity-specific gotchas surfaced today, all visible in Swarm2BdaBehaviour.RenderCorrFrame:

  • Inactive descendants. The tank prefab kept the mesh-bearing child Tank at activeInHierarchy=False in tac-map mode. The render path now recursively walks descendants, records which were inactive, sets them active for the render, and restores in finally.
  • LOD bias. The cam pushes close to the contact, but the LOD group still picks the lowest detail because the camera was previously far away. QualitySettings.lodBias = 100f for the render duration forces max detail.
  • Bounds vs pivot. The prefab's pivot is at ground level; the tank centre is +1.2 m up. Aim the cam at renderer.bounds.center, computed across all renderers including the just-woken inactive ones.

Why a second INVEST sometimes appears mid-engagement

The behaviour at the seam is simple once you draw the two loops:

  1. Swarm3ReconBehaviour sweeps continuously and publishes contacts on the bus. Outgoing contacts are deduped in a per-agent cache with a short TTL (proximity + heartbeat — see EMCON throttle).
  2. Swarm2InvestigateBehaviour on every Swarm 2 agent listens to the bus. On HandleContactReceived, idle agents run a closest-idle election. One wins and self-tasks.
  3. KillChainCoordinator mediates the operator's authorisation and the strikers — but does not gate recon. Recon doesn't know an auth has been granted; the strike loop and the recon loop run independently.

While the strikers are inbound, the tank is still alive. Recon's next pass classifies it again, the dedupe TTL has expired (or a different recon agent makes the call), and a fresh contact goes on the bus. Idle Swarm 2 agents that weren't picked the first time around — possibly the same UAV-09 that just finished its first investigation cycle and reset to Idle — run the election again. The closest one wins, and starts flying to a stand-off. By the time it arrives, either the wreck is on the ground (and it'll either corroborate the kill or clear out) or it isn't, and the operator has a fresh sensor in position.

This is correct concurrent behaviour. The doctrine says: until a target is observed destroyed, keep watching. Two strike windows, two sets of eyes — that is the swarm's structural advantage over a single-engagement weapon.

The only place this surfaces a UI question is operator perception. A second INVEST bar in the timeline can read as “new threat” rather than “same threat, second cycle.” A small, low-risk improvement is to dim or pale-shade INVEST markers whose contact is within proximity of an active FireAuthRecord. The behaviour stays unchanged; the timeline just communicates intent more clearly.

Why BdaReceipt is the canonical close-out

The original close-out was an aggregate: the chain closed when every striker's StrikeOutcome had landed in the record. This worked for HIT-only outcomes. The day someone added StoodDown as a self-aborted outcome, an awkward case appeared: a stand-down agent sometimes never publishes its outcome at all because it never arrived at its strike geometry — it just turned around. Aggregate-based close-out hung indefinitely. The dialog's CLOSE button never appeared.

The BDA receipt pivot makes close-out causally simple: the chain closes when somebody outside the strike says the engagement is over. If BDA confirms destroyed, outcome is Destroyed. If BDA times out (never showed up), outcome is Missed. Either way the operator gets a CLOSE button within a bounded window. Per-UAV outcomes are still tracked and displayed, but they are diagnostic, not the close-out condition.

Audit firehose ↔ timeline UI

Each behaviour calls a single static helper:

AuditSwarmEvent(agentId, "Swarm2InvestigateBegin",
    "begin " + contactId,
    string.Format("standoff={0} accepted={1}", standoff, accepted));

The helper appends a SwarmAuditEntry to a process-static ring buffer. MissionTimelineTab on Update reads new entries since its last cursor and emits coloured bars. The switch (entry.EventType) in MissionTimelineTab is the only place colour and label are decided — adding a new marker type means one line in the audit caller and one case in the switch. Greg added the entire kill-chain row of markers (RECON, INVEST, CORR, LOCK-ON, FIRE-REQ, GRANTED, DENIED, LAPSED, BDA RTE, HIT, STAND-DOWN, BDA, BDA TIMEOUT) in a single commit.

The ring buffer caps at 10 000 entries. The publish gate that mirrors new audit entries onto the DDS AuditLog topic compares the current buffer count against the last-published count — and that comparison stops triggering once the cap is hit. The original implementation used AuditLogCount for the gate, which goes flat at 10 000 and never grows again, so after roughly four minutes of swarm activity the CommsLog tab stopped receiving new audit entries entirely. Thomas's 2026-05-08 demo bit on this — the kill chain ran, but operators saw nothing new on any CommsLog screen. The fix is a separate monotonic counter, AuditTotalWritten, that the gate diffs against. It never wraps; the publisher reads the new tail of the ring buffer each cycle. Single line of state, no schema change on the wire.

Post-2026-05-09 hardening — what shipped

The hardening between the original 2026-05-09 ship and this revision concentrated on the seam costs the demo surfaced:

  • Side-cache lifecycle. The contact-thumbnail cache (recon thumbs + CORR thumbs keyed by (contactId, agentId)) lives on KillChainCoordinator. v1 was an unbounded static dictionary — every demo session leaked the recon Texture2Ds until the editor scene reloaded. Now the cache is bounded: 50-entry cap with 120 s TTL, oldest evictions Object.Destroy(tex) the Texture2D explicitly, and a 5 s periodic prune sweep gated on Time.time. OnDestroy also walks the dictionary and destroys everything so editor play-mode stops clean up too.
  • Task-status publish at 1 Hz heartbeat + change-detected. The cross-bus task-status publisher used to fire unconditionally at 5 Hz for every active vehicle. Most cycles re-published the same (TaskId, Status, Progress) fingerprint. The publisher now compares each fingerprint to the previous cycle and skips writes when nothing changed, then force-emits everything once per second so late subscribers and dropped-sample recovery still work. Saved roughly 80% of the steady-state writes during a typical demo. The entire block is gated on EmconState so the same publisher silently bypasses the wire when EMCON is asserted.
  • Lapse-on-extinction fallback. Greg's third end-state for a fire-auth (AUTH-LAPSED) needs to fire even when every granted striker dies before any of them ticks the lapse from inside its own behaviour. Dead behaviours can't tick. So KillChainCoordinator.TickBdaWatchdog walks the active records at 1 Hz, and any record that's Granted with zero strikes executed and zero of its GrantedAgentIds still alive in the bootstrap raises LapseFireAuth directly. Operator sees the dialog transition to Cancelled/Lapsed within a bounded second instead of staying stuck on Granted forever.

What you would change next

  • Render second-cycle INVEST markers in a paler shade when their contact overlaps an active FireAuthRecord's footprint (UI-only, low-risk).
  • Replace the aggregate stand-down counter with a per-UAV state stream (some strikers' outcomes still arrive after the close-out — they update the record but do not move it back to Pending).
  • Make the contact + CORR thumbnails in FireRequestDialog click-to-expand. All three currently set raycastTarget=false; the side-cache already holds the Texture2D so the work is in the UI layer (one click handler, one overlay panel).
  • Promote EmconBurstQueue from the Phase 1 minimal (slot pick + delayed publish) to full Option D — random backoff in window/2 .. window×2, retry counter, JSON per-vehicle config, collision-detection counter.
